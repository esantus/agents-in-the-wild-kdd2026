Agents in the Wild:
Where Research Meets Deployment

ACM Paper →

Abstract

Agentic systems — large language model (LLM)-based architectures capable of reasoning, planning, acting, and coordinating with tools and other agents — are rapidly transitioning from research prototypes to production-scale deployments across domains such as software engineering, scientific discovery, and finance. While academic work has emphasized benchmarks and algorithmic innovation, deployment raises new challenges around robustness, safety, and reliability.

This tutorial brings together researchers and practitioners to explore advances in reasoning and planning, multi-agent coordination, and evaluation, highlighting open challenges arising from deployment experience. Through applied case studies in pharmaceutical discovery and financial systems, we analyze common design patterns that make agentic systems successful, and discuss practical mitigation strategies for failure modes, such as verification pipelines, fallback mechanisms, and human-in-the-loop supervision. Attendees will gain a comprehensive view of the field along with concrete design patterns, evaluation checklists, and templates for safe and reliable deployment across industries.

What You Will Learn

  • A clear understanding of the evolution of agentic systems — from single-LLM prompting to modular and multi-agent orchestration.
  • An introduction to modern reasoning, planning, and execution strategies, including their trade-offs and representative examples.
  • Evaluation techniques beyond static benchmarks, emphasizing robustness, reliability, safety, and continuous adaptation.
  • Concrete case studies from deployment domains (e.g., bioinformatics pipelines, financial assistants) illustrating common failure modes and effective recovery strategies.
  • Awareness of open research challenges and opportunities at the intersection of academic innovation and industrial practice.

Tutorial Program

Part I — Foundations

3.1
Agentic Systems: History and Definitions

From single-model pipelines combining prompting, tool use, and heuristic control to modular, multi-agent architectures. The evolution of reasoning, planning, execution, and memory components.

3.2
Agent Foundations: Reasoning, Planning & Multi-Agent Coordination

Task decomposition, multi-plan generation, iterative reflection, memory-augmented planning. Architectural topologies, communication protocols, and fault-tolerance mechanisms for teams of agents.

3.3
Retrieval and Reasoning Pipelines

Iterative, adaptive, and modular retrieval strategies that interleave reasoning and information lookup for multi-step, dynamic tasks.

3.4
Evaluation Beyond Benchmarks

Dynamic, behavior-centric evaluation frameworks. Scenario-driven robustness, behavioral safety, and trustworthiness. Concrete analyses of failure modes: hallucination, deadlocks, drift, and cascading errors.

Part II — Applied Perspectives

4.1
Agents in Pharmaceutical and Life Sciences

End-to-end autonomous scientific discovery spanning literature synthesis, hypothesis generation, and experimental validation. Multi-agent frameworks for drug candidate identification and biomedical discovery.

4.2
Agents in Finance

LLM-based agents evolving from static analytical tools to dynamic decision-support systems. Planner–executor–verifier architectures for earnings call summarization, portfolio allocation, and risk assessment.
